## Conflicts the worker reported in the lecture itself

- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet says the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve perforates the cricothyroid membrane and gives sensory innervation to the subglottic stage. Standard teaching is that the external laryngeal nerve is motor to the cricothyroid and that sensation below the vocal folds comes from the recurrent laryngeal nerve, which the packet also states elsewhere. “Innervates the cricothyroid muscle and perforates the cricothyroid membrane; sensory innervation of the subglottic stage” — section “Blood supply, lymphatics and innervation”; “Recurrent laryngeal nerve supplies all intrinsic muscles except the cricothyroid, and sensation below the cords” — exam:10
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet describes the respiratory-type laryngeal epithelium as 'cylindric stratified'. Standard teaching names respiratory epithelium pseudostratified ciliated columnar, which is the wording one of the packet's own question options uses for the rest of the larynx. “The epithelium is respiratory type (cylindric stratified) throughout” — section “Topographical anatomy and histology”; “Ciliated pseudostratified columnar type — Lines the rest of the larynx” — q:mk4jvs
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet lists the three acoustic parameters as frequency, intensity and pitch, with pitch depending especially on the shape of the supralaryngeal cavities. In standard teaching the parameter shaped by the resonating cavities is timbre (quality), while pitch is set by the frequency of vocal fold vibration, which the packet itself says elsewhere. “pitch, depending on the accommodation of the vocal folds but especially on the shape of the supralaryngeal cavities” — section “Physiology of phonation”; “pitch depends upon the frequency with which the vocal folds vibrate” — [slide 64](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-larynx/64.jpg)
- **internal**: The packet says all the intrinsic muscles adduct the vocal cords except the posterior cricoarytenoids, but elsewhere says the cricothyroid tenses the cords rather than adducting them and that the thyroarytenoid relaxes them. “All of them adduct the vocal cords except the posterior cricoarytenoids, which abduct them” — section “Muscles”; “The cricothyroid tenses the cords rather than adducting them” — q:1e6wd2n
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet says the posterior laryngeal vein drains to the inferior thyroid vein and then the subclavian vein. Standard teaching is that the inferior thyroid veins drain into the brachiocephalic veins. “Posterior laryngeal vein to the inferior thyroid vein, then the subclavian vein” — section “Blood supply, lymphatics and innervation”
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet names the thickenings of the thyrohyoid membrane the medial thyroid ligament and lateral thyroid ligaments. The standard names are the median thyrohyoid ligament and the lateral thyrohyoid ligaments. “with a medial thickening (medial thyroid ligament) and two lateral thickenings (lateral thyroid ligaments)” — section “Joints and ligaments”

## Audit findings

- `undefined` — **unsupported**: The definition makes the superior and inferior thyroarytenoid 'parts' of the one thyroarytenoid muscle that relaxes the cords. The packet lists them as two separate constrictors and never says they are parts of the 'Thyroarytenoid' of the action table; the inferior one is the vocal muscle, which the packet says tenses the cord (and which the result itself gives as an alias of the vocalis concept). Neither evidence quote states the parts.
- `undefined` — **wrong-alias**: 'Rima glottidis' is the standard name of a different, narrower thing: the opening between the vocal cords, which is how the packet's own question defines it. The concept is defined as the compartment at the level of the vocal cords, and the result makes the vocal fold part-of it, so it is not the gap. The packet's loose use should have gone under conflicts, not aliases.
- `undefined` — **medically-doubtful**: The definition says the cricothyroid ligament is stretched between the thyroid cartilage and the vocal process. Standard teaching: the cricothyroid ligament (conus elasticus) rises from the upper border of the cricoid; only its free upper margin, between the thyroid angle and the vocal process, is the vocal ligament, and its median part joins cricoid arch to thyroid cartilage. The packet itself gives the cricoid-to-thyroid version elsewhere. The result mentions this only in notes; it is not under conflicts, so nothing is listed in affects.
