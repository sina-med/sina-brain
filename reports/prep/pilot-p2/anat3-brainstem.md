## Conflicts the worker reported in the lecture itself

- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: Slide 20 says the trochlear nerve (CN IV) innervates the 'major oblique muscle' and 'ensures upward gaze'. Standard teaching is that the trochlear nerve supplies the superior oblique muscle, which depresses (and intorts) the eye, i.e. downward gaze. “Nerve IV (pathetic nerve: the only one that emerges on the posterior surface of the brainstem innervates the major oblique muscle of the eye: ensures upward gaze)” — [slide 20](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/20.jpg)
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet lists the cephalic flexure and the mesencephalic (cranial) flexure as two separate bends, and slide 4 places the cephalic flexure between brainstem and spinal cord. In standard teaching the cephalic flexure is the mesencephalic flexure (in the midbrain region), and the bend between hindbrain and spinal cord is the cervical flexure. “the cephalic flexure: brainstem and spinal cord.” — [slide 4](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/04.jpg); “The growing neural tube bends at the cephalic flexure and the mesencephalic (cranial) flexure.” — section “Introduction and embryology”
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet says the medial eminence marks the midline of the floor of the fourth ventricle (one question option calls it the 'median eminence'). In standard teaching the midline is marked by the median sulcus, with a medial eminence on each side of it; 'median eminence' is the standard name of a hypothalamic structure. “The medial eminence marks the midline of the floor.” — [slide 15](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/15.jpg); “The median eminence marks the midline.” — q:zdor62
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: Slide 20 calls cranial nerve XI 'the spinal nerve', while slide 17 calls it the accessory nerve. The standard name is accessory nerve; 'spinal nerve' is the standard name of the nerves arising from the spinal cord, so it was not listed as an alias. “XI the spinal nerve” — [slide 20](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/20.jpg); “cranial part of accessory(11th)” — [slide 17](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/17.jpg)
- **internal**: Slides 15 and 17 carry the heading 'DESCRIPTIVE ANATOMY: THE MIDBRAIN' but describe the floor of the fourth ventricle (pons and medulla) and the nerves emerging from the medulla; the summary places this content under the pons and medulla. “DESCRIPTIVE ANATOMY: THE MIDBRAIN Posterior Surface The floor of the fourth ventricle is composed of the dorsal surface of the pons and the medulla.” — [slide 15](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/15.jpg); “DESCRIPTIVE ANATOMY: THE MIDBRAIN Nerves emerging from Medulla (4 nerves):” — [slide 17](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/17.jpg)

## Audit findings

- `undefined` — **medically-doubtful**: The definition calls cranial nerve XI 'one of the mixed nerves', which the packet does say (it groups IX, X and XI as 'the mixed nerves'). Standard teaching is that the accessory nerve is purely motor; only IX and X of that group are mixed. The result does not report this under conflicts, so nothing lists concept:accessory-nerve as affected.
- `undefined` — **medically-doubtful**: The packet supports 'the brainstem connects above to the diencephalon by the transverse fissure of Bichat', but in standard teaching the transverse cerebral fissure (of Bichat) is a cleft separating the cerebral hemispheres from the diencephalon and brainstem; it does not join the midbrain to the diencephalon, which are directly continuous. A fissure is also not a connecting structure, and the packet's own question says it 'separates'. Not reported under conflicts.
- `undefined` — **wrong-alias**: The alias 'crus cerebri' is the standard name of a different thing: the anterior part of the cerebral peduncle only (the peduncle also includes the substantia nigra and tegmentum). The result's own notes admit this, yet it is listed as an alias and not reported under conflicts as the instructions require.
- `undefined` — **evidence-does-not-support**: The definition says the middle peduncle is 'the largest of the three', resting only on the parenthesis in hy:4, which follows the word 'pons' and may equally mean that the pons is the largest part, as hy:1 says with the same wording. No other block says the middle peduncle is the largest.
