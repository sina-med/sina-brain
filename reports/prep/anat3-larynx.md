## Conflicts the worker reported in the lecture itself

- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet says the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve perforates the cricothyroid membrane and gives sensory innervation of the subglottic stage. Standard teaching is that the external laryngeal nerve is motor to the cricothyroid, and that sensation below the vocal folds comes from the recurrent laryngeal nerve, as the packet itself states elsewhere. “External branch | Innervates the cricothyroid muscle and perforates the cricothyroid membrane; sensory innervation of the subglottic stage” — section “Blood supply, lymphatics and innervation”; “below = recurrent laryngeal” — hy:4
- **internal**: The summary says bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve damage leaves both cords paramedian, but a question's option note describes both cords fixed in abduction as bilateral palsy. “bilaterally, both cords lie paramedian with loss of phonation and difficulty breathing” — section “Clinical correlations”; “Both cords become fixed in abduction  — That is bilateral palsy.” — q:ciq1vx
- **internal**: For unilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve damage, one place says the other cord compensates and phonation is not much affected, while a high-yield point lists a hoarse voice. “usually the other cord compensates and phonation is not much affected” — card:anat3-larynx/l32; “Unilateral recurrent laryngeal palsy (thyroidectomy, left-sided aortic or lung lesions): hoarse voice, cord paramedian” — hy:6
- **internal**: The packet gives two accounts of pitch: one says it depends on vocal fold accommodation but especially on the shape of the supralaryngeal cavities, another says pitch depends on the frequency of vibration. In standard teaching pitch corresponds to frequency, and the quality shaped by the resonating cavities is timbre. “pitch, depending on the accommodation of the vocal folds but especially on the shape of the supralaryngeal cavities” — section “Physiology of phonation”; “Loudness depends on the amplitude of vibration, pitch on the frequency” — section “Physiology of phonation”
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet describes the respiratory-type laryngeal epithelium as cylindric stratified. Standard teaching is pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium; a question's option note in the packet also uses that term for the rest of the larynx. “The epithelium is respiratory type (cylindric stratified) throughout” — section “Topographical anatomy and histology”; “Ciliated pseudostratified columnar type  — Lines the rest of the larynx.” — q:mk4jvs
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet places the upper limit of the adult larynx at C4. Standard teaching usually gives the adult larynx as extending from C3 to C6. “Larynx extends from the upper thyroid cartilage at C4 to the lower cricoid at C6” — exam:3
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet says the posterior laryngeal vein drains to the inferior thyroid vein and then the subclavian vein. Standard teaching is that the inferior thyroid veins drain to the brachiocephalic veins. “Posterior laryngeal vein to the inferior thyroid vein, then the subclavian vein” — section “Blood supply, lymphatics and innervation”

## Audit findings

- `undefined` — **unsupported**: The packet never describes one thyroarytenoid muscle 'with superior and inferior parts' running from the reentrant angle to the arytenoid; it lists a superior thyroarytenoid and an inferior thyroarytenoid (vocal muscle) as separate constrictors, and separately says 'thyroarytenoid' relaxes the cords. The definition merges them from outside knowledge, so its inferior part is the same muscle the result's vocalis concept says tenses the cord; the two quotes given state only the relaxing action.
