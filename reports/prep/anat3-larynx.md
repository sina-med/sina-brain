## Conflicts the worker reported in the lecture itself

- **internal**: Pitch is listed as a third acoustic parameter separate from frequency, depending especially on the shape of the supralaryngeal cavities, but the same section later says pitch depends on the frequency of vibration of the vocal folds. “pitch, depending on the accommodation of the vocal folds but especially on the shape of the supralaryngeal cavities” — section “Physiology of phonation”; “Loudness depends on the amplitude of vibration, pitch on the frequency” — section “Physiology of phonation”
- **internal**: The muscles section says all the intrinsic muscles adduct the vocal cords except the posterior cricoarytenoids, but a question explanation says the cricothyroid tenses the cords rather than adducting them. “All of them adduct the vocal cords except the posterior cricoarytenoids, which abduct them” — section “Muscles”; “The cricothyroid tenses the cords rather than adducting them” — q:1e6wd2n
- **internal**: The histology text calls the respiratory-type laryngeal epithelium cylindric stratified, while a question's option note says ciliated pseudostratified columnar epithelium lines the rest of the larynx. “The epithelium is respiratory type (cylindric stratified) throughout” — section “Topographical anatomy and histology”; “Ciliated pseudostratified columnar type  — Lines the rest of the larynx” — q:mk4jvs
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet gives the external branch of the superior laryngeal nerve sensory innervation of the subglottic stage, whereas elsewhere it assigns sensation below the cords to the recurrent laryngeal nerve, which is also the standard teaching; the external branch is usually taught as motor to the cricothyroid. “perforates the cricothyroid membrane; sensory innervation of the subglottic stage” — section “Blood supply, lymphatics and innervation”; “Recurrent laryngeal nerve supplies all intrinsic muscles except the cricothyroid, and sensation below the cords.” — exam:10

## Worker's notes

- Slides 37 and 64 are scanned textbook pages with heavy OCR errors and missing words; slide 53 is only figure labels. Quotes from them are copied as they appear, errors included.
- The packet uses 'vocal cord' and 'vocal fold', 'vestibular fold', 'ventricular band' and 'false vocal cord', and 'cricothyroid ligament' and 'cricothyroid membrane' interchangeably; each group is treated as one concept with the other terms as aliases.
- 'Lymphatic drainage of the larynx' is classed as a process for want of a better kind; it describes the distribution and destination of the laryngeal lymphatics.
- In q:ciq1vx the note on distractor A ('Both cords become fixed in abduction — That is bilateral palsy') does not say whether it means bilateral recurrent palsy alone, for which the text gives the paramedian position, or combined recurrent and external nerve damage; not listed as a conflict.
- The text says lymphatics are absent at the vocal cords but also that the anterior commissure, where the cords unite, contains vessels and lymphatics; reported here rather than as a conflict.
- Several figure questions and cards (hyoid bone, trachea, laryngeal prominence) are supported mainly by the question or card text itself, since the lecture sections mention these structures only in passing.
