## Conflicts the worker reported in the lecture itself

- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet lists a cephalic flexure and a separate mesencephalic (cranial) flexure, and the slide places the cephalic flexure between brainstem and spinal cord. Standard teaching: the cephalic flexure is the mesencephalic flexure (at the midbrain); the bend between hindbrain and spinal cord is the cervical flexure, and a pontine flexure also exists. “The growing neural tube bends at the cephalic flexure and the mesencephalic (cranial) flexure.” — section “Introduction and embryology”; “the cephalic flexure: brainstem” — [slide 4](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/04.jpg)
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: A slide says the trochlear nerve supplies the 'major oblique' muscle and 'ensures upward gaze'. Not used in the output. “one that emerges on the posterior surface of the” — [slide 20](https://sina-med.github.io/files/slides/anat3-brainstem/20.jpg)
- **differs-from-standard-teaching**: The packet equates the cerebral peduncle with the crus cerebri and divides the midbrain into tectum and cerebral peduncles only. Standard teaching: the crus cerebri is only the anterior part of the cerebral peduncle, which also includes the tegmentum (and substantia nigra). - **internal**: The summary, exam point and slide give the blood supply as the basilar artery and its branches only, while a high-yield point adds the vertebral system. Standard teaching is that the brainstem is supplied by the vertebrobasilar system, the medulla mainly by the vertebral arteries; also the posterior choroidal artery, listed as a basilar branch, is normally a branch of the posterior cerebral artery. “Supplied by the basilar artery and its branches.” — exam:14; “Blood supply = basilar (+ vertebral) system.” — hy:10
